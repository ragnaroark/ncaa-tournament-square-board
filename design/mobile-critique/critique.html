Square Board Mobile Critique
Design critique · mobile

NCAA Square Board on a phone

How the current 10×10 squares board holds up for participants checking their own square and following results on a phone, what to fix first, and a working mockup of the proposed mobile layout.

Reviewed: index.html, styles.css, script.js at commit f308bc5Rendered at 375, 390 and 412 px wide, plus landscape63 games, 100 squares

Overall: the board is a faithful, compact port of the desktop page, and it fits a phone screen without sideways scrolling. The biggest opportunity is that the page is still organised around the grid instead of around the two things a participant on a phone actually wants: find my square and what it has paid, and what just happened. Three mechanics also fight the phone directly: a scroll gesture that starts on the board opens a popup, the newest game is the last of 63 items inside a nested scroll area, and the round colours are too faint to tell apart.

First impression

The eye lands on the grid, which is the right object, but the grid says little on its own: pale tints, tiny names, and no sense of what any square has earned. The second thing the eye finds is the 'View All Winnings' button crowding the title. Nothing on the first screen answers "did my square win?" or "who won the last game?". The purpose is clear to someone who already knows squares pools; it is not clear what to tap.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the grid's black borders and the header digits. That is the right object, but the grid carries no money or recency information, so the eye has nowhere to go next.
  • Reading flow: title → button → pills → board → legend → games. The two things the user wants, their own square and the latest result, are not on this path. A Fit board with 31px cells is a map, not a reading surface; it needs a summary strip above it and a sheet below it.
  • Emphasis: the strongest colour on the page is the orange "active game" highlight, which is almost the same hue as the Sweet 16 tint and the championship gold. Money, the thing everyone cares about, is a small green number on each card.
  • Board cells: the ID in the corner, the name, and a diagonal split of two or three tints compete inside 32px. At that size a cell can carry one fill and a row of dots, not a gradient.

Touch targets (44×44px recommended)

  • Board cells: 31–33px square at 375–390px wide. A 10-wide grid cannot reach 44px on a phone, so the board needs a Large mode with horizontal scroll, and the names need a second route (search, sheet).
  • Round pills: 29px tall. Popup close ×: 24×26px. Legend items are not tappable although they look like filters.

Text readability

  • Cell names render at 6.4px (0.4rem) below 400px and 8px at 576px. Anything under 11px is decoration on a phone.
  • Legend text is 10.4px; axis label 8.8px; the row axis label is hidden entirely on phones, so "losing score" is explained only by "L ↓" in the corner.

Semantics

  • Cells are divs with click handlers, so they are not reachable by keyboard or announced as buttons. The popup has no role="dialog", no aria-modal, and focus stays on the page behind it.
  • Round filters are radio inputs inside labels, which is good; they only need visible focus styles.

What works well

  • The board fits the screen width at every phone size with no horizontal page scroll, and the shuffled header digits are kept large and legible.
  • Pill-style round filters on mobile are the right pattern; they just need to fit.
  • The square popup leads with Total Winnings in a strong, readable block and lists wins highest round first.
  • Every game card shows the winning square number, which is the key link between results and board.
  • Click and touch handlers exist everywhere, so nothing is mouse-only.
  • Game data is isolated in games.js, so a redesign of the view does not touch the data entry workflow.

Proposed mobile layout

The mockup below is a working page built from the real board names and all 63 results. It keeps the shuffled digit headers and the payout schedule and reorganises the phone experience around three tabs. Open it on a phone, or narrow the window, to use it full-bleed.

Open the interactive mockup
  • "Your square" strip pinned under the header with the running total; set from any square's sheet and remembered on the device.
  • Round segmented control that doubles as the payout schedule: seven equal cells with R64 $100 through Champ $4,000.
  • Name search that highlights matching cells and lists them with totals, so four MTAC squares become four tappable chips.
  • Board with one solid tint per cell for its highest round, a row of dots for each win, an orange corner for your square, and a Fit / Large toggle. Large mode shows full names at 56px cells with horizontal scroll.
  • Bottom sheet for square details: scroll-locked, Escape and swipe to dismiss, total at the top, each win with its round, score and payout.
  • Results tab newest first, grouped by day, each card showing both scores, the winning square owner and the payout. Tapping opens the sheet without moving the page.
  • Leaders tab with pot total, squares paid, ranked list with a payout bar segmented by round, and your square highlighted.

Priority recommendations

  1. Stop scrolls from opening popups. Remove the touchend handlers on cells, backdrop and buttons and rely on click. This is a one-line change per handler and removes the most disorienting behaviour on the page.
  2. Put "my square" and "latest result" on the first screen. Add name search with match highlighting, a saved "my square" strip with its total, and sort results newest first with the inner scroll area removed. These three changes serve both user goals without redesigning the grid.
  3. Replace tints and gradients with one legible round palette. Six distinct hues at full strength for dots and chips, lighter fills for cells, used identically in the legend, filters, cards and sheet. Reserve green for money and orange for "yours".
  4. Turn the popup into a bottom sheet. Scroll lock, Escape, swipe to close, role="dialog", total at the top, one close control. Open the same sheet from game cards instead of scrolling the page.
  5. Fit the filters and show the money. Seven equal segments with payout underneath, and a "+$" amount on every game card.
  6. Fix the small stuff while in there. Contrast on green, grey and the dash; 44px targets for pills and close; reduced-motion guard on the championship glow; cap cell size in landscape; make cells real buttons.
Measurements come from Chromium with touch emulation at 375×667, 390×844 and 412×915 (device pixel ratios 2 to 3) and 844×390 landscape. Contrast ratios follow the WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance formula with alpha tints composited on white.
